The process in one minute

What is compression moulding?

A measured amount of material, called the charge, is placed into an open, heated mould. A press closes the mould and squeezes the material until it fills every corner of the cavity. Heat and pressure are held while the material cures (thermosets) or vulcanises (rubber). Then the mould opens and the part comes out.

It is the process behind car body panels and bumpers in sheet moulding compound (SMC), electrical housings in bulk moulding compound (BMC), rubber seals, melamine tableware, and the pressing stage of many ceramic tiles.

  1. ChargeWeigh and place the material in the cavity.
  2. HeatThe mould halves are held at curing temperature.
  3. PressThe press closes and the material flows to fill the shape.
  4. CureHeat and pressure are held while the part sets.
  5. DemouldThe mould opens, the part is ejected and the flash trimmed.

Why go

Why Turkey?

Turkey combines a large manufacturing base with thousands of years of pressed, fired and moulded craft, all within a few hours of each other.

Automotive and composites

Bursa and the Kocaeli industrial belt east of Istanbul are centres of Turkey's vehicle and supplier industry, where moulded composite and rubber parts are everyday business.

Ceramics and tiles

From the historic tiles of Iznik to the ceramics town of Kutahya, see how clay is shaped, pressed, glazed and fired, from museum pieces to modern production.

Trade fairs

Istanbul hosts major plastics, composites and manufacturing shows, a practical way to meet many suppliers in a single day.

Heritage and daily life

Imperial mosques, covered bazaars, tea gardens and regional food are never far from the industrial zones.
